\section{Distance between samples}\label{distance-between-samples}

Based on \texttt{20260909}, the first pass results will be based on the
pairwise sample distances, reported as the Mahalanobis distance with the
Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance estimator. The family-wise averages of
the randomized dichotomy distances and the pairwise sample distances
seem to be well correlated, so the overall results should be similarly
reliable.

\section{Reliable site distributions}\label{reliable-site-distributions}

Figure~\ref{fig-corrs} is a copy of the empirical CDFs and histograms of
the split-half correlations for the sites, originally calculated in
\texttt{20260826}. Previous results looked at the results for sites with
correlations between 0.3 and 0.6.

Split-half correlations in V1 and IT are similarly shaped, with a
right-skewed distribution and a mode around \(\rho \approx 0.1\). For
V2, the correlations are left-skewed. V4 has a somewhat bimodal
distribution, with a local peak around \(\rho \approx 0.1\) and
\(\rho \approx 0.6\).

Data reflects split-half correlations performed when all blanks are

somewhat bimodal distribution for low- and high-correlations.}

\section{Pairwise samples compared with randomized
dichotomies}\label{pairwise-samples-compared-with-randomized-dichotomies}

Pairwise sample comparisons are done separately for each family.

Visually, the distributions between the pairwise sample comparisons and
the randomized dichotomies look similar, though the magnitudes of the
distances are affected by the different number of samples
(Figure~\ref{fig-rd_vs_ps}). The orderings on family-separated results
are also similar (these plots are saved but not rendered here). When
plotting the within-family averages and standard deviations against each
other (Figure~\ref{fig-rd_vs_ps_corr}), the correlations tend to be good
for within-family averages and worse for within-family standard
deviations (Table~\ref{tbl-rd_vs_ps}).

To make this more rigorous, one could test for similarity between the
relative rankings of region performance with a post hoc test. However,
this is probably overkill for now.

I am fairly unworried about big differences between the results from the
pairwise sample distances and the randomized dichotomies. Subsequent
analyses will be performed on the pairwise sample distances, which will
save time (and memory).
